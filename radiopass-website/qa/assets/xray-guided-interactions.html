FRCR traps
  • Photoelectric absorption is not restricted to the K shell; K-shell interactions are important above the K-edge.
  • The incident photon is completely removed in photoelectric absorption.
  • Compton involves a loosely bound electron whose binding energy is negligible relative to photon energy.
  • The scattered Compton photon has lower energy.
  • Iodine's high Z and useful K-edge favour photoelectric absorption.
  • Compton predominates in soft tissue at CT energies.
Probability rules
  • Photoelectric probability rises strongly with atomic number and falls rapidly with energy.
  • Compton depends mainly on electron density and much less on atomic number.

Photoelectric approximately proportional to Z3 / E3.
Compton decreases slowly with energy and follows electron density.

Clinical meaning
  • Photoelectric absorption improves subject contrast, especially for iodine and bone, but contributes to absorbed dose.
  • Compton scatter reduces contrast and contributes to staff exposure.
  • Higher kVp increases penetration and generally reduces photoelectric subject contrast.
  • Collimation, grids and air gaps reduce scatter reaching the detector.
