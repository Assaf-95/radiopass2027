Free Induction Decay

After a 90° RF pulse, magnetisation tips into the transverse plane. Spins precess at Larmor frequency and induce an oscillating MR signal. Its amplitude decays exponentially due to spin-spin interactions and static field inhomogeneity. This observed decay is T2* (always shorter than true T2 when inhomogeneity is present).

Role of 180° Pulse

The 180° refocusing pulse reverses dephasing from static field inhomogeneity (T2′ contribution), but cannot reverse intrinsic spin-spin relaxation. Therefore, echo recovery restores static dephasing losses but still follows true T2 amplitude decay.

Spin Echo Timing

A spin echo sequence uses one 90° pulse and one 180° pulse. The 180° is placed at TE/2. Echo maximum occurs at TE. Connecting successive echo peaks produces a true T2 envelope, largely free from static inhomogeneity effects.

TR and k-Space Repetition

For spin echo imaging, the pulse sequence is repeated once per k-space line. The interval between successive 90° excitations is TR. Longer TR allows more longitudinal recovery before the next excitation.

Simultaneous Relaxation

Transverse relaxation produces measurable MR signal decay, while longitudinal relaxation restores magnetisation along B0. Both occur at the same time throughout the repetition period.

Weighting Rules

In spin echo, TR mainly controls T1 weighting and TE mainly controls T2 weighting. Short TR/short TE: T1 weighted. Long TR/long TE: T2 weighted. Long TR/short TE: proton density weighted.
