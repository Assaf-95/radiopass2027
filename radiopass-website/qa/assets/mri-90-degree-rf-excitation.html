THE TIP THAT MAKES THE SIGNAL

MR signal formation begins with a radiofrequency pulse tipping the net magnetisation away from the longitudinal axis. In the laboratory frame it does not swing across. It spirals.

SCROLL ↓
01 — EQUILIBRIUM

At rest along B₀

Left alone in the bore, the proton population settles. Slightly more spins align with the field than against it, and their vector sum — the net magnetisation M₀ — sits quietly along z.

This longitudinal component is not directly detectable by the standard receiver coil. The coil detects magnetic flux that is changing, and a vector aligned with z does not sweep through it.

M₀ ∝ N · γ²ℏ²B₀ / 4kT
02 — RF ON

A second, smaller field

The transmit coil produces B₁, a field lying in the transverse plane and rotating at exactly the Larmor frequency — 63.87 MHz at 1.5 T.

It is tens of thousands of times weaker than B₀. It works because it is resonant: it turns in step with the precession, so its small push accumulates coherently over repeated revolutions.

ω₀ = γB₀ = 42.58 MHz/T × 1.5 T
03 — NUTATION

The spiral down

M now precesses about B₀ and tips about B₁ at the same time. The result is not an arc — it is a helix, winding down the surface of a sphere.

In a real 1.5 T pulse of a few hundred microseconds it makes tens of thousands of turns on the way down. Slowed here to six, or you would see nothing but a blur.

α = γ · B₁ · τ
04 — ROTATING FRAME

Now stop spinning

Step onto a turntable rotating at ω₀ and B₀ vanishes from the maths. What is left is B₁, standing still along x′ — and M simply tips over it like a hinge.

Watch the helix unwind. Same physics, same pulse, same 90°. This is why every textbook draws the rotating frame and almost never the lab frame.

B_eff,z = B₀ − ωRF/γ = 0 at resonance
05 — SIGNAL

All of it, transverse

At 90° the longitudinal component is gone and the entire magnetisation lies in the transverse plane, sweeping past the receiver coil.

That sweep induces a voltage. The voltage decays as the spins dephase, and that decaying oscillation — the free induction decay — is the raw signal underlying MR image formation.

S(t) ∝ M₀ · sin α · e^(−t/T2*)
