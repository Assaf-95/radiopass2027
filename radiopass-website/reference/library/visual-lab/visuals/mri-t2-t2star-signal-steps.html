2. Spin-Spin Relaxation

Free Induction Decay

Once the RF pulse stops, each nucleus slightly alters the local magnetic field.

As a result, some nuclei precess slightly faster and others slightly slower.

Since precessional frequency depends on magnetic field strength, these small variations cause nuclei to gradually lose phase coherence.

As coherence is lost, the net transverse magnetisation decreases exponentially to zero.

This decay is called the Free Induction Decay.

T2 Constant

The rate of transverse magnetisation loss is determined by magnetic interactions between neighbouring spins.

This process is known as spin-spin relaxation or transverse decay.

The time constant governing this decay is called T2.

Definition of T2

T2 is the time required for transverse magnetisation to decay to 37 percent of its original value. In other words, it has lost 63 percent of its maximum signal.

T2 depends on the local magnetic field environment.

Factors Affecting T2

Bone, calcium, and metal
Have short T2 values. Rigid solids produce large local magnetic field variations.

Fat
Has a short T2.

Water
Has a very long T2. Rapid molecular motion smooths local magnetic field variations, prolonging coherence.

T2 Star (T2*) and Free Induction Decay

The previously described T2 decay represents the ideal exponential transverse decay.

In reality, transverse magnetisation decays faster than expected.

This accelerated decay is called T2 star (T2*).

T2* includes the effects of:

  • Local magnetic field variations
  • External magnetic field inhomogeneities

Although the main magnetic field (B0) is highly uniform, it is not perfectly homogeneous. Tissue differences and materials within the scanner further distort the field.

These inhomogeneities cause faster dephasing of nuclei.

Spin Echo and Correction of T2*

The spin-echo sequence removes the effects of magnetic field inhomogeneities. This allows measurement of the true tissue-specific T2 relaxation rather than T2* effects.
