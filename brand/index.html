RadioPass

A mark for doctors who read the invisible. Minimal enough for a watch dial, with a second meaning only a radiologist will catch.

The idea

The window, before the diagnosis.

The first act of every read is windowing — choosing which greys become visible. The boundary inside the lens is that exact curve: flat, ramp, flat — the window/level transfer function every FRCR Part 1 candidate sketches, applied a thousand times a day for a career.

To everyone else it is simply a beautiful object: a lens, a monocle, an eclipse of gold. To your registrars it is recognition.

And quietly: the lit and dark halves are exactly congruent — rotate one half a half-turn and it becomes the other. Two exams. One preparation.

RadioPass is already the name your sites, code and materials carry; it is shorter in a browser tab, and the wordmark sits tighter beside the lens. Radiology Pass reads more formally but sacrifices the compactness a mark this quiet deserves.

The study

Six directions it beat.

Seven marks were drawn independently, rendered at full and favicon size, and scored blind by three judges — an identity craftsman, an FRCR examiner, and a production realist. Window/Level won on craft, small-size integrity and honest radiology.
